Library

I decided to keep track of the books I have read. I will keep expanding this list as I remember more of them.

Fiction

  • The Hobbit — J. R. R. Tolkien. I read it as a child after my cousin got a copy. Made me quite excited to read The Lord of the Rings, although I never did.
  • Robinson Crusoe — Daniel Defoe. Probably the book I have read the most times—seven or eight. I was quite obsessed with it as a child.
  • The Belgariad(series) — David Eddings. Another favorite from when I was a boy.
  • War and Peace — Leo Tolstoy. I enjoyed it, although it was honestly a little too long.
  • The Brothers Karamazov — Fyodor Dostoevsky. Like the most Ivan's later conversation with the Devil felt like a strong influence on The Master and Margarita.
  • The Master and Margarita — Mikhail Bulgakov. An interesting political satire with fantastical elements.
  • Daphnis and Chloe — Longus. I expected a much more archaic reading experience from ancient Greece, but its opening is evocative and immediately feels surprisingly modern.
  • The Satyricon — Petronius. It was in the same collection as Daphnis and Chloe, still a bit weird to read a novel from so long ago.
  • The House with the Mezzanine and Other Stories — Anton Chekhov. Chekhov often recycles same characters such as misunderstood artists and provincial doctors, but he is still my favourite writer.
  • Use of Weapons — Iain M. Banks. Banks is a great writer and very pleasant to read. I rushed through it too quickly because of that.
  • The Forever War — Joe Haldeman. Great and to the point
  • Foundation series — Isaac Asimov. I like history books so naturally enjoyed its future pseudo history style
  • Consider Phlebas — Iain M. Banks.
  • The Player of Games — Iain M. Banks.
  • The Plague — Albert Camus.
  • Rendezvous with Rama — Arthur C. Clarke.
  • Ubik — Philip K. Dick.
  • The Name of the Rose — Umberto Eco.
  • Shadow & Claw — Gene Wolfe.
  • Sword & Citadel — Gene Wolfe.
  • Starship Troopers — Robert A. Heinlein.
  • Neuromancer — William Gibson.
  • 1984 — George Orwell.
  • Animal Farm — George Orwell.
  • Burmese Days — George Orwell.
  • A Clergyman's Daughter — George Orwell.
  • Keep the Aspidistra Flying — George Orwell.
  • Coming Up for Air — George Orwell.

Non-fiction

Programming

  • 100 Go Mistakes and How to Avoid Them — Teiva Harsanyi. I did not get to write much Go by hand before LLMs arrived oh well. Recommended.
  • Scala for the Impatient — Cay S. Horstmann. A useful introduction that helped me get started.
  • Multithreaded JavaScript — Thomas Hunter II and Bryan English. I learned some things that I never got to use outside interviews.
  • Designing Data-Intensive Applications — Martin Kleppmann. The author's lectures on the subject are available here.
  • Domain Modeling Made Functional: Tackle Software Complexity with Domain-Driven Design and F# — Scott Wlaschin.
  • Database Internals — Alex Petrov.
  • Software Architecture: The Hard Parts — Neal Ford, Mark Richards, Pramod Sadalage, and Zhamak Dehghani.
  • Dependency Injection Principles, Practices, and Patterns — Mark Seemann and Steven van Deursen.
  • Functional Design and Architecture — Alexander Gran.

History

  • Guns, Germs, and Steel — Jared Diamond. A classic; I even gave it to a friend as a present.
  • Economics For Dummies — Sean Masaki Flynn. A useful overview, although I unreasonably expected something more historical and in-depth. I will need to read something else to hold my own at parties.
  • An Area of Darkness — V. S. Naipaul. I enjoyed Naipaul's writing, and India is an interesting .
  • India: A Wounded Civilization — V. S. Naipaul. Another book about India.
  • 1812: Napoleon's Fatal March on Moscow — Adam Zamoyski. Very nicely written. The attention to Polish episodes was cute, and it gave me a more historical overview of War and Peace.
  • The Old World in the New — Edward Alsworth Ross. An archaic sociology book from the early twentieth century, but I still liked the author's style.
  • The Huns — Hyun Jin Kim. I didn't like author's prose.
  • Fire and Blood: A History of Mexico — T. R. Fehrenbach.

Other

  • Man and His Symbols — Carl G. Jung. It seems that thinking a lot about dreams gives you really cool dreams. Jung's paintings are interesting too.
  • Storm of Steel — Ernst Jünger. Surreal in its brutality. I need to read more by Jünger to see how his life developed after the war.
  • Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy — Joseph Schumpeter.
  • The Origin of Consciousness in the Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind — Julian Jaynes.
  • Industrial Society and Its Future — Theodore Kaczynski.
  • The Road to Wigan Pier — George Orwell.
  • The Red Queen: Sex and the Evolution of Human Nature — Matt Ridley.
